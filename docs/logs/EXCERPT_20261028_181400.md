   - The channel layout {0/1: left woofers, 2: left tweeter, 3/4: right woofers, 5: right tweeter} is **CORRECT**.
2. **Why was 100 Hz completely silent in plain stereo (`SpeakerDsp=0`)?**
   - In raw/plain stereo without EQ, a tiny 16-inch laptop woofer diaphragm has an extremely steep physical acoustic roll-off below ~150–200 Hz.
   - At endpoint volume 0.2 with amplitude 0.3 and `OutputAttenuation=1` (-6 dB), the physical signal sent to the speaker is -30 dBFS.
   - According to the human equal-loudness curve (ISO 226 / Fletcher-Munson), human ear sensitivity at 100 Hz is ~30 dB lower than at 1 kHz. A 100 Hz tone at -30 dBFS on tiny laptop cones falls below the audible room noise floor, while 1 kHz and 8 kHz ring out clearly.
   - This validates Claude's theoretical finding: the un-EQ'd plain path leaves mids and highs +13 to +23 dB louder than bass.
   - **Conclusion:** To hear bass on this chassis, the woofers require the KAIT2EN DSP profile (`SpeakerDsp=1..4`), specifically Bankstown Virtual Bass (which synthesizes 2nd and 3rd harmonics in the 200–450 Hz band) and the 12 biquad stages in `g_T2DspWoofer`.
